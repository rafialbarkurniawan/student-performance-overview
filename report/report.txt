================================================================================
STUDENT PERFORMANCE DASHBOARD ANALYSIS REPORT (PORTUGAL, 2008)
================================================================================

Headline Metrics (Academic Performance Overview):
- Student Records: 649
- Average Final Grade: 11.91
- Average Absences: 3.66
- Average First Period Grade: 11.40

1. Average Final Grade Across Schools
   - Question: Does the average final grade differ across schools?
   - Findings: Yes. On the overview page, Gabriel Pereira shows a higher
     average final grade than Mousinho da Silveira. In the school summary
     table and drill-through detail pages, Gabriel Pereira (423 records) has
     an average final grade of 12.58, whereas Mousinho da Silveira (226 records)
     has an average final grade of 10.65 (a difference of 1.93 points).

2. Final Grade Patterns by Study Time Category
   - Question: How do final grade patterns behave across study time categories?
   - Findings: Across the overview and both school detail views, average final
     grades generally follow a downward pattern as weekly study time decreases:
     - "5 to 10 hours" and "More than 10 hours" sit at the highest levels and
       are closely matched (with "5 to 10 hours" slightly ahead or virtually
       tied with "More than 10 hours").
     - "2 to 5 hours" sits lower than the two higher study-time groups.
     - "Less than 2 hours" records the lowest average final grade across the
       categories shown.

3. Relationship Between Previous Class Failures and Final Grade Differences
   - Question: Are prior class failure records associated with differences in
     final grades?
   - Findings: Yes, higher numbers of previous failures correspond to lower
     average final grades across the views. In Gabriel Pereira's detail view,
     "No Previous Failure" is near ~13, "1 Previous Failure" is around ~10,
     "2 Previous Failures" is around ~9, and "3 or More Failures" is around
     ~8.6. In Mousinho da Silveira's detail view, "No Previous Failure" is
     around ~11.5, while the remaining failure categories drop into the ~7–8.5
     range (note that bar ordering in the failure chart differs slightly across
     views, so check labels carefully).

4. Variation in Absences Across Schools
   - Question: Is there variation in absences across schools?
   - Findings: Yes. Overall average absences are 3.66 across 649 records.
     Gabriel Pereira has a higher average absence count (4.22 on its detail
     page; ~4.2 in the overview table) compared to Mousinho da Silveira (2.62
     on its detail page; ~2.6 in the overview table).

5. Value of Drill-Through for Context Behind Summary Figures
   - Question: Does drill-through help users understand the context behind
     summary figures?
   - Findings: Yes. While the overview page aggregates both schools together
     (e.g., 649 records, 11.91 average final grade, and 3.66 average absences),
     drilling through to each school's detail page isolates that school's record
     count, average final grade, average absences, failure-category breakdown,
     and study-time breakdown—clarifying how each school contributes to the
     overall averages.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KEY ANALYTICAL LIMITATIONS & CAVEATS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Geographic scope: Data comes from two schools in Portugal and cannot be
  generalized to the entire student population.
- Data collection method: Data was collected through school records and
  questionnaires rather than a real-time educational monitoring system.
- No unique student ID: Unique student IDs are not available; row counts must
  not be claimed as verified unique individuals.
- Correlation vs. causation: Associations among study time, absences, class
  failures, and grades do not prove cause and effect.
- Grade period correlation (G1, G2, G3): G3 is the final grade, whereas G1 and
  G2 are earlier-period grades. UCI specifically notes a strong correlation among
  them; do not treat G1 and G2 as independent evidence that other factors caused
  changes in G3.
